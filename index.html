About

Research statement

I design miniaturized antennas and wireless links that let medical implants communicate safely from inside the human body. My doctoral research at Tampere University focuses on intracranial implants: multi-band implantable antennas, implant-to-implant communication through realistic head tissue, and the SAR and temperature-rise limits that determine how much power such links may use.

I work across the full chain, from antenna design and full-wave electromagnetic modeling to prototype fabrication and measurements in tissue-mimicking phantoms. Before my PhD I worked in industry as an antenna design engineer and as an RF simulation application engineer, and that shapes how I approach research: designs should be buildable, measurable and useful.

    Research themes

    My work runs from antenna design and full-wave EM modeling to prototype fabrication and experimental validation.

    Miniaturized implantable antennas

    Compact multi-band antennas for intracranial implants, built on flexible and biocompatible materials and characterized in multilayer head models.

    Figure placeholder

    Implant-to-implant communication

    Near-field coupling and link budgets between implants in realistic human-body scenarios, validated with prototypes in liquid head phantoms.

    Figure placeholder

    RF exposure, SAR & thermal safety

    SAR and temperature-rise assessment that sets the maximum safe transmit power and checks compliance with exposure guidelines.

    Figure placeholder

    Water-based antennas

    Antennas that use water as a radiating or tuning medium, aiming for structures that adapt well to biological environments.

    Figure placeholder

    EM–thermal multiphysics modeling

    Coupled electromagnetic and thermal simulation linking antenna performance to tissue heating in anatomically realistic models.

    Measurement methods

    A custom antenna measurement setup with independent control of height, spacing, axial rotation and planar orientation for repeatable link experiments.
